## Context

One section across different uses: a family of table, floor, wall and pendant lights.

## My role

BENWU team · 3D design & visual presentation.

## Design problem

As length, direction and support change, which formal characteristics hold a product family together?

## Design decisions

### Build identity through the section

A near four-lobed end profile and longitudinal light strip create common features, while length, orientation and support vary.

![Silver table lamp with a circular base beside an open book on a blue surface](/works/legacy/lighting/5beb6f4875965136b4cf16ffcfe6f1fe.webp)

### Explain material and function in the detail

Metal channels, luminous surfaces and circular controls provide distinct visual cues for construction, light and interaction.

![Detail of a gold light body with longitudinal light strips and a circular control](/works/legacy/lighting/e9ab15f955279470fd53cb6e9e9a8c18.webp)

### Calibrate proportion in context

Desks, walls and long tables provide references for comparing how one linear language adapts to different uses.

![Gold vertical wall light within a grey architectural recess](/works/legacy/lighting/3f7ec7c3d1a16952bb9a0a0c1523be7b.webp)

## Outcome & stage

Object, detail and interior views make the family’s formal, material and proportional relationships available for comparison.

## Reflection

Visual consistency does not establish shared engineering components. Further development requires optical, thermal, assembly and control validation.
