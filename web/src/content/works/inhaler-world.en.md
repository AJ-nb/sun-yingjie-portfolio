## Concept

**A SMALL INHALER & A WIDER WORLD** is the organising idea. The inhaler becomes an entry between two worlds: a product held at hand and an imagined landscape built from sky, sea, mountains, classical architecture and Renaissance figures. A quiet, small action is enlarged into a sensory experience of opening more space.

## Visual language

Surrealist collage reconstructs reality by placing incompatible elements together. This series translates that logic into a product language through tears, scale shifts, spatial penetration, figure–product interaction and impossible perspective. Blue sky and the product’s green recognition colour suggest air, clarity and release. Classical painted texture sits beside contemporary product photography to create a deliberate time contrast.

## Narrative structure

The sequence moves from approaching the entry, through the entry, and into the wider world. The product first appears as a held, floating or cropped object, then crosses the image boundary and interacts with figures, arches, mountain faces and water. The shared portrait ratio, colour anchor and light direction let the posters read as one system rather than isolated images.

## Design decisions and observed result

The central decision was to keep the product recognisable while allowing the surrounding scale to break open. The inhaler is not treated as an isolated packshot; it works as a hinge between a hand gesture, a classical opening and a distant landscape. Photographic material and shadow preserve object credibility, while collage jumps turn “breathing” from a functional action into a spatial sensation.

## Contribution, boundary and next steps

I set the concept direction, visual narrative, image selection, sequence, copy and public-display boundary. The posters are supplied final images; the page does not infer their capture process, model provenance, commercial release or product performance. A next iteration could extend the language into motion posters, packaging unfoldings and a physical window system using the same entry, tear and scale-shift rules.
