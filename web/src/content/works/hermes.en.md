## Context

Contributing to three seasonal window narratives through merchandise, props, material and light.

## My role

BENWU team · 3D design & visual presentation.

## Design problem

How can each season create a distinct spatial world while keeping merchandise at the centre of the composition?

## Design decisions

### Summer: graphic energy in space

Graphic shapes, colour blocks and repetition establish rhythm. Product-to-background contrast keeps the merchandise legible.

![Summer window with a fishing rod, folding chair, fish props and a speech bubble](/works/legacy/hermes/40379d8484144b6a435498fcf8b9d857.webp)

### Autumn: a surreal domestic world

Familiar furniture and objects become narrative elements through shifts in scale and unexpected spatial relationships.

![Autumn window with an oversized pink fish passing through a doorway beside a mannequin](/works/legacy/hermes/043e019544984416521bf2009313a5fb.webp)

### Winter: an equestrian frozen landscape

The horse, snow planes and suspended accessories establish a hierarchy of figure, merchandise and background.

![Wide winter window with a large white horse head, snowy planes, a yellow bag and a small pink horse](/works/legacy/hermes/f1455674fa1906fe49bd555bcad97e9c.webp)

## Outcome & stage

The project record retains summer, autumn and winter spatial and visual studies. My contribution focused on 3D design and visual presentation within the BENWU team.

## Reflection

Seasonal distinction involves more than colour and decoration. Viewing distance, merchandise hierarchy, prop proportion and the response of materials to light are the useful comparisons.
