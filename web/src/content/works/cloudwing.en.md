## Background

CloudWing explores a future VTOL passenger aircraft through the relationship between body surfaces, access and seating space. The proposal focuses on the visual expression of overall form and cabin experience.

## My role

This is a collaborative project. The material includes sketches, body proportions, a canopy, cabin, landing supports and scene renders.

## Key question

The continuous body surface must make sense beyond its closed state. Canopy opening, passenger access and deployed supports need clear relationships. A complete silhouette and legible use states are parallel form-design questions.

## Design process

Sketches first compare body and cabin proportions. Multiple views then explain the canopy, interior and landing supports. Color and scenarios explore visual character, while open-state views add information unavailable in the closed silhouette.

This retrospective concerns access and cabin representation, without inferring aerodynamics from smooth surfaces. Propulsion architecture, balance and practical access need separate engineering evidence.

## Final work

The final board presents continuous body surfaces, cabin access and passenger space, with multiple render angles describing the overall appearance.

## Outcome and stage

The outcome is an aircraft form concept. Propulsion architecture, aerodynamics, flight and safety have not been validated; the images do not represent a flying prototype.
